RESERVATION / THE HIDDEN RULE

The reservation rule nobody told you about

Same merit list. Same quotas. A different counting rule can produce a very different result.

You must have seen screenshots like this going viral.

But do you know the mathematical impossibility hiding behind the cutoff debate?

Under the current rule, a reserved cutoff can never be higher than the Open cutoff.

You are going to learn something new today. Swipe left. →

01 / QUICK QUESTION

How many reserved seats are left?

An exam has 100 vacancies, of which 50 are reserved for specified categories. The first 50 candidates selected purely on merit rank list include 30 general-category candidates and 20 reserved-category candidates. How many of the 50 reserved vacancies remain?

02 / THE REVEAL

All 50 are still left

Under the current rule, the 50 Open seats are filled first.

So the first 50 candidates in the rank list fill those Open seats. This includes 20 reserved-category candidates.

Those 20 candidates do not use any reserved seats. The 50 reserved seats are then filled by another 50 reserved-category candidates.

30 General 70 reserved-category

Final list: 30 General candidates and 70 reserved-category candidates.

The reservation percentage was 50%. The final representation became 70%.

Not a software error. This is the rule.

04 / THE MISSED DETAIL

The detail both camps miss

We argue about reservation every day.

Almost always as “for” or “against”.

Almost never about how seats are counted.

Most elected representatives, loud public voices and judges do not know this counting rule or its extreme consequences.

Yet this small rule decides real outcomes.

We rarely test the extreme result before accepting the rule.

05 / CONSEQUENCE

Why the cutoffs cannot cross

Allocation ready.

Open takes the highest scores first. After it is full, General candidates are skipped while reserved seats fill from lower ranks.

If you were as naive as me, you may have thought the cutoffs would move closer once every community was adequately represented.

Stop waiting. The current rule makes crossing impossible.

06 / CONSEQUENCE

What happens over time?

Take 10 seats. Five are Open and five are reserved.

Earlier, only one candidate from the community reached the top five. After years of reservation, let's say three candidates do now.

The community now gets three Open seats and the same five reserved seats. Total: eight out of ten.

The two extra achievements are added on top of the quota, not counted inside it.

Here is my proposal. Some call this counting rule a minimum guarantee.

Over and above: The first 50 are selected in Open. Then 27 OBC seats and 23 SC/ST seats are filled separately. Final: 14 General, 51 OBC and 35 SC/ST.

Horizontal allocation: OBC and SC/ST achievements count towards their minimums. The top 100 already cross both minimums. Final: 28 General, 47 OBC and 25 SC/ST.

Same merit list. Very different result.

Simplified illustration: 50.5% Open, 27% OBC, and 22.5% combined SC/ST. The population mix is only for this example. It is not from a current caste census.

10 / CORNER CASE

Take it to the extreme

100%

This is a mental exercise, not a forecast.

Suppose reserved-community candidates win every Open seat.

Every selected person can now belong to a reserved community.

At this extreme, the basic representation and participation argument for reservation crumbles. A rule meant to prevent exclusion can produce a list that excludes every General candidate.

11 / POPULAR REFORMS

Creamy layer does not change this arithmetic

Creamy layer, family limits and voluntary surrender can change who receives a reserved seat.

They may help the benefit reach a poorer family or someone lower in the category list. But Open is still filled first.

1A candidate becomes ineligible for a reserved seat because of creamy layer, a family limit or voluntary surrender.
2If their rank is high enough, they can still win an Open seat.
3Another eligible candidate still fills the reserved seat.

This changes who gets the reserved seat. It does not create another Open seat for a General candidate.

12 / THE FEEDBACK LOOP

Publish numbers people can understand

A reserved cutoff hides the category’s best performers because they were already counted under Open.

Want to know whether performance is improving? Publish these four numbers after every exam:

12 of 39selected OBC candidates came through Open
27 of 39selected OBC candidates came through OBC seats
8 of 31selected SC/ST candidates came through Open
23 of 31selected SC/ST candidates came through SC/ST seats

Numbers from this illustration. They are not results from a real exam.

Cutoffs alone cannot show whether the policy is working.

13 / HOW IT STARTED

This was a legal choice

The Constitution allows reservation. It does not print this step-by-step allocation method.

  1. 1989The Union says reserved-category candidates selected on merit will not be counted against reserved seats.
  2. 1992Nine judges uphold this rule in Indra Sawhney.
  3. 1995R. K. Sabharwal confirms the rule for government jobs.
  4. 1996Ritesh Sah applies the same rule to medical admissions.
  5. 2020Saurav Yadav: Open belongs to everyone.

This is not a portal bug. It is a deliberate legal rule. Its extreme result is rarely discussed in public.

14 / PROCESS IS NOT POLICY

The candidate does not choose

A common misconception is that candidates choose after the exam whether to use Open or reservation.

That is not how it works.

COMMON MYTHCandidate chooses Open or quota after the exam✕
  1. 1Open fills first by rank
  2. 2High-ranked reserved candidate goes into Open
  3. 3The community quota remains untouched

The candidate does not choose to save the quota. The rule does it automatically.

Checking certificates earlier will not change this. Only changing the counting rule will.

15 / THE LEGAL ROUTE

Changing it will not be easy

ROUTE 1 Supreme Court

Indra Sawhney had nine judges. To reverse it through the courts, a larger bench would be needed, most likely eleven judges.

ROUTE 2 Constitution

Parliament would need to amend the Constitution. Governments would then need to rewrite admission and recruitment rules.

An amendment must pass each House with a majority of its total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting. It can still face basic-structure review.

A portal update cannot override this doctrine.

16 / THE QUESTION WE MISSED

Now ask the right question

“Do we need reservation?” may not be the right question. Start with the rule.

When 27 seats are reserved for a community, should its Open winners count inside those 27? Or should they be added on top?

Same ranks. Different rule. Different final list.
